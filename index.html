Important - Count Time

  • Count time means the prison pauses entry and exit and suspends movement, which causes delays and prevents admission. It takes 60 minutes or more to clear.
  • No processing of attorneys or visitors from 3:00 p.m. until the count clears (9:00 a.m. on Saturdays, Sundays, and federal holidays). Attorneys who arrive within an hour before a count will not be able to visit their client until the count clears.
  • Exclusive attorney visiting hours: Monday - Friday, 8:00 a.m. - 12:00 p.m. There are no social visits during these hours.
  • Legal visits during these hours do not need to be scheduled in advance.
  • Special legal visits outside these hours: request through MDC Brooklyn's Legal Department with at least two business days' notice. Email BRO-LegalVisit-S@bop.gov.

Visiting Guidelines

What to wear and what to expect. Visitors can be denied entry for inappropriate clothing, so plan ahead.

Families & Friends

What to wear

Dress as you would for a large gathering of men, women, and young children.

Do not wear
  • Anything green, or khaki / military-style clothing (looks like inmate clothing)
  • Shiny buttons
  • Underwire bras
  • Sweatshirts, hoods, or coats (they can hide contraband)
  • Hats or caps
  • Tight shirts (shirts for women should not be too tight)
  • Revealing, see-through, or low-cut clothing, including crop tops, halter tops, backless tops, sleeveless garments, leotards, spandex, and bathing suits
  • Revealing shorts, miniskirts, or skirts two inches or more above the knee
  • Dresses or skirts with a high-cut slit in the front, back, or side

Rules can vary by facility. When in doubt, choose something loose, plain, and modest.

Attorneys

What to wear

All visitors, including attorneys, must dress appropriately for a correctional environment.

Do not wear
  • Khaki or tan pants (same color as inmate clothing)
  • Hoods or coats (they can easily hide contraband)
  • Provocative or revealing clothing

The duty officer has the final say on whether attire is appropriate inside the visiting room.

Good to know

Visiting duration

By law, an inmate gets at least four hours of visiting time per month, and the prison can often provide more. The Warden can limit the length of visits or the number of visitors at once to prevent overcrowding.

General behavior

Visits must be quiet, orderly, and dignified. The visiting room officer can require you to leave if you or the inmate is not behaving appropriately.

Physical contact

Handshakes, hugs, and kisses (in good taste) are usually allowed at the start and end of a visit. Staff may limit contact for security reasons. Conjugal visits are not permitted.

Set up CorrLinks email

People at MDC Brooklyn can send and receive monitored email through CorrLinks. You cannot start the conversation: the person inside has to add you first.

  1. The person adds you as a contact. You receive an email with a link and an identification code.
  2. Use the link to accept (or block) the request.
  3. Register using the same email address that got the invitation, plus the identification code.
  4. Check your email for a verification link, then sign in to send and receive messages.

Open the setup instructions (PDF)

This guide was written for Oregon's system, so its first steps and any fees may not match MDC Brooklyn. The invitation and registration steps work the same way.

Email (TRULINCS)

  • Most people at MDC Brooklyn have access to email, which staff monitor.
  • Accounts are set up through TRULINCS and need prior approval.
  • Recipients must first respond to the initial email to confirm they want to receive emails from a federal inmate.
  • Recipients can accept or block all future electronic communication from the person.

Incoming mail

  • Mail is processed on a 5-day schedule (Monday - Friday).
  • All correspondence is photocopied, and the person only receives the photocopy. Special mail and legal mail envelopes are not photocopied.
  • MDC will reject: commercial greeting cards; anything with glitter, stickers, lipstick, crayon, or marker; oily substances or fragrances.

Outgoing mail

  • Address it properly and include the person's full name, register number, and full return address, including MDC Brooklyn.
  • It is reviewed beforehand and unsealed.
  • Staff may randomly read it and seal it.

Legal mail

  • Mark the envelope "Special mail - open only in the presence of the inmate" so it is not opened and stays confidential.
  • The sender should identify themselves and their firm or organization on the envelope.
  • All legal and special mail packages are X-rayed before entry.
  • Packages are opened in front of the person.

Phone Calls

Calls with attorneys

  • Pretrial inmates can use unmonitored phones in the housing units and call the Federal Public Defender's Office of the Southern and Eastern Districts directly, free of charge.
  • People with private attorneys use the regular inmate telephone, which is recorded.
  • Attorney calls are not allowed for social reasons.

Unmonitored attorney calls

  • An occasional unmonitored call to an attorney is allowed. These calls fall under the special mail provisions for confidentiality.
  • Frequent confidential calls are only allowed when the person shows that correspondence, visiting, and normal telephone use are not adequate, for example because of an imminent court deadline. Once that is shown, the Warden cannot limit how often they call their attorney.
  • To get an unmonitored call, the person or their attorney must request approval from the Unit Team and the Legal Department.

Emergency calls

Emergency calls are only for the death or serious illness of an immediate family member.

  • Request the call through the Religious Services Department or Unit Management.
  • To reach someone for an emergency, call the main number (718-840-4200) and ask for the Religious Services Department, Unit Management, or the appropriate Unit Team.

Getting Married While Incarcerated

How it works

  1. The person submits a written request to marry through their case manager. It goes to their unit team.
  2. The unit team reviews the request and sends a written report of its findings and recommendation to the Warden.
  3. The Warden decides and notifies the person in writing. A copy goes in their central file.
  4. If the request is denied, the decision can be appealed through the Administrative Remedy Procedure.

Requirements

Legally eligible If the person is or was married, written verification that the marriage was legally dissolved is required.
Mentally competent The person must be mentally competent to marry.
Spouse confirms The intended spouse must verify their intention to marry, usually in writing.

The Warden approves the request unless there is a legal restriction, or the marriage would threaten the security or good order of the institution or the protection of the public.

All marriage expenses, such as the marriage license, are paid by the inmate, their intended spouse, their family, or another source approved by the Warden.
